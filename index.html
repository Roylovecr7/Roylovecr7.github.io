About me

I am an M.Sc. candidate in Applied Psychology (Engineering Psychology) at Shaanxi Normal University, supervised by Prof. Xiangling Zhuang. I am originally from Suzhou, China.

My research lies at the intersection of psychology, human factors, and human–computer interaction. I use immersive simulation, behavioral experiments, and multimodal measurement to investigate how people perceive, interpret, and respond to intelligent systems. My work has primarily focused on pedestrian interactions with automated vehicles, while also extending to age-friendly voice interfaces and other human–technology interactions.

My current research focuses on human–automated vehicle interaction, particularly the psychological and physiological processes underlying perceived safety, comfort, trust, and cooperation. In the longer term, I am interested in human factors across HCI and human–machine systems, with the broader goal of understanding how intelligent systems can be designed to support human performance, experience, and safety.

I am currently seeking Ph.D. opportunities in human factors, human–computer interaction, and intelligent transportation systems for Fall 2027.
